# The Unification Constitution
## A Living Order for Truth Across Domains

## Preamble

Unification is not achieved when every field is forced to speak one vocabulary. It is achieved when the truths discovered in different fields can meet without lying about what they are.

Physics must remain physics. Mathematics must remain mathematics. History must remain history. Theology must remain theology. Moral judgment must remain accountable to the reality of persons, harm, truth, love, and responsibility.

The purpose of this constitution is to govern their relation.

It exists so the framework does not become a machine that converts every question into “coherence,” every disagreement into a defect, or every theological confession into counterfeit science. It exists so the framework can grow, be corrected, survive attack, and still tell one truthful story.

The question is not merely whether the pieces fit.

The question is whether they are allowed to fit honestly.

---

## Article I - Jurisdiction

Every domain retains authority over the kinds of claims it is actually equipped to establish.

- Physics governs physical models, measurements, conservation, dynamics, and empirical prediction.
- Mathematics governs derivation from stated axioms and definitions.
- Information theory governs formal information quantities and implemented communication constraints.
- History governs testimony, documents, provenance, and inference about past events.
- Theology governs confession, doctrine, Scripture, worship, ultimate purpose, and the identification of God, sin, grace, Christ, resurrection, and new creation.
- Moral reasoning governs responsibility, harm, obligation, justice, mercy, and the treatment of persons.

No domain may annex another merely because it has powerful language.

Physics cannot declare theology false by failing to measure God as an object inside creation. Theology cannot declare a physical equation true because it makes a beautiful spiritual picture. Mathematics cannot make a premise true by proving what follows from it. History cannot prove a universal law from one event. Moral urgency cannot substitute for evidence.

The domains are not enemies. They are jurisdictions.

---

## Article II - The Bridge Is a Public Act

No claim may cross from one domain into another by insinuation.

Every bridge must state:

```text
What is being mapped?
From which domain to which domain?
What structure is preserved?
What is not preserved?
What assumptions make the map possible?
What would count against the map?
What kind of warrant does the map have?
```

A bridge is not a poetic resemblance. It is not an excuse to borrow the authority of physics for a theological sentence or the authority of theology for an empirical sentence.

It is a public act of translation.

The framework may say that entropy offers a structural grammar for decay, that information offers a grammar for signal and corruption, or that Christian grace names restoration from beyond exhausted self-resources. It may not silently turn these grammars into identities without a stronger bridge.

---

## Article III - Separation of Powers

Every major claim has three powers that must not be merged.

1. **Evidence:** What does the native domain actually establish?
2. **Interpretation:** What broader structure may the result participate in?
3. **Identification:** What does the Christian account say that structure finally means?

Evidence without interpretation becomes a pile of disconnected facts.

Interpretation without evidence becomes fantasy.

Identification without confession becomes a disguised theology that refuses to say its own name.

The three belong together. None is allowed to impersonate the others.

---

## Article IV - The Burden of Translation

The burden belongs to the person making the bridge, not to the critic refusing it.

The framework does not receive credit merely for finding the same word in different fields. It must show why the proposed correspondence is more than an analogy, or label it as analogy and proceed honestly.

The greater the claim, the greater the burden:

```text
Resemblance requires an example.
Structural correspondence requires a map.
Identity requires bidirectional preservation.
Physical prediction requires an operational measurement and a failure rule.
Christian identification requires theological and historical argument.
```

No amount of elegance cancels this burden.

---

## Article V - Rights of the Real

Reality has rights against the framework.

- A native result may veto a preferred interpretation.
- A failed prediction may narrow or kill a physical claim.
- A rival model may explain a pattern better.
- A person may not be reduced to a score, a coordinate, a data point, or a moral caste.
- A victim's suffering may not be explained away as a convenient variable in someone else's theory.
- A theological mystery may remain a mystery rather than being filled with decorative mathematics.

The framework is allowed to make bold claims. It is not allowed to make reality answer to them.

---

## Article VI - Negative Control and Equal Hearing

Every central route needs an alternative that fails for a reason.

That is why random-order law paths matter. That is why rival frameworks must face the same socket tests. That is why a model must say what it cannot explain.

No theory may judge itself by a standard it refuses to apply to IIT, global-workspace theory, free-energy approaches, process theology, naturalism, generic theism, or any other serious rival relevant to the claim.

The framework does not fear a rival winning one question. A theory that cannot survive a fair hearing does not deserve to govern the whole.

---

## Article VII - Subsidiarity

The smallest adequate level should do the work.

A question about channel capacity should first be answered with channel mathematics. A question about a historical event should first be answered with historical method. A question about a person's moral responsibility should not be swallowed whole by cosmology. A question about salvation should not be reduced to a laboratory instrument.

Unification begins only where a local answer reaches its real boundary and a bridge is necessary.

This prevents the framework from becoming totalitarian in thought. The whole serves the parts; it does not erase them.

---

## Article VIII - Amendment and Memory

The constitution is living because it can be corrected, not because it can be bent to protect a preferred conclusion.

Every amendment must preserve:

- the previous statement;
- what changed;
- why it changed;
- what source, test, or argument forced the change;
- what claims are superseded;
- what remains unresolved.

The framework must remember its mistakes. A correction log is not embarrassment. It is proof that truth matters more than winning.

---

## Article IX - Public Descent

Every important claim must have a Monday-morning version.

If a claim cannot be said plainly, tested against ordinary life, and distinguished from manipulation, it has not completed its public responsibility.

Public language is not a lesser form of thought. It is the final test that the framework has not hidden its weakness inside jargon.

---

## Article X - The Moral Purpose

The framework is not built to sort humanity into good people and bad people.

The line between good and evil runs through every human heart. Good is not a tribal possession. Evil is not an equal creative force. Good creates, keeps truth, protects life, and makes relation possible. Evil corrupts what it did not create.

Therefore the purpose of unification is not domination. It is truthful orientation:

```text
preserve truth
protect life
repair relation
name corruption without becoming corrupt
hold justice and mercy together
remain open to correction
```

Christian theology gives this purpose its fullest name: the Logos grounds truth; grace restores what fracture cannot heal; Christ is not an ornament on the system but the claimed resolution toward which the system points.

---

## The Constitutional Test

Before a claim enters UNIFICATION, ask:

1. Which jurisdiction owns the native claim?
2. Is a bridge actually needed?
3. Is the bridge public, typed, and graded?
4. What could veto it?
5. What rival receives equal hearing?
6. Can it be said plainly?
7. Does it preserve truth, life, relation, justice, mercy, and the dignity of persons?

If the answer fails, the claim is not yet ready to govern the whole.
