# Doctrinal Grounding — Master Equation Domain

## The Trilemma and Atonement Doctrine

### The Problem (Systematic Theology)
Christian theology has long recognized the tension between God's
justice and God's mercy. The Reformers framed it as: How can God
be just AND the justifier of the ungodly? (Romans 3:26)

The framework contribution is showing this is not merely a
theological tension — it is an algebraic impossibility in any
closed system. J + M = 1 is not a metaphor. It is arithmetic.

### Atonement Theories (how the church has understood the solution)

**Penal Substitution (Reformed):** Christ bears the penalty.
T_ext = D. The cost is real and fully absorbed. This maps
directly to the algebraic resolution.

**Christus Victor (Patristic):** Christ defeats the power
holding humanity captive. This addresses the adversarial
decoherence — the active opposition, not just the passive debt.

**Moral Influence (Abelard):** Christ's sacrifice transforms
the offender's will. This addresses W — how free will is
preserved in the restoration without coercion.

**Recapitulation (Irenaeus):** Christ re-walks human history
and succeeds where Adam failed. This is the coherence
restoration narrative — returning χ to pre-Fall values.

### The Framework's Contribution
The framework does not adjudicate between atonement theories.
It shows they are DIFFERENT PROJECTIONS of the same algebraic
resolution, each emphasizing a different variable:

- Penal Substitution emphasizes T_ext (cost transfer)
- Christus Victor emphasizes the adversary (active decoherence)
- Moral Influence emphasizes W (free will transformation)
- Recapitulation emphasizes χ (coherence restoration)

They are not competing. They are the same solution seen from
different domains. That itself is a Theophysics result.

### Honest Caveat (from GPT Codex)
The algebra strongly models the structural problem. It does
not by itself prove the Trinity. What it can do is show that
Christian doctrine uniquely claims a solution-type that closed
human systems cannot generate. That is already very strong.
But it is a structural argument, not a proof of existence.
