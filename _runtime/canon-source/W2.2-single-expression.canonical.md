# The Single Expression

Space, matter, motion, time — not a sequence. One created act, read four ways.

Part One — The Fullness Speaks

## Out of Simplicity, Everything

Sheet 000 ended with the Fullness — the _plērōma_ , Paul's own word for everything God is, complete, nothing missing. "For it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell." Colossians 1:19; 2:9 Creation begins when the Fullness speaks outward. And the direction of that act settles an old question before it is asked: simplicity breeds complexity. Never the reverse. Fractions do not create unity; parts do not sum to a ground. The simple is prior to the composite — always. Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I q.3 a.7

So the first act cannot be an assembly. It must be an expression — one thing said, that unfolds as many. And that is exactly how the first breath of Scripture reads:

In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. Genesis 1:1–2

Read it with the fundamentals in hand. _In the beginning_ — time, granted first, set apart grammatically from the things made: not an object alongside heaven and earth, but the _when_ He gives for them to run in. _The heaven_ — space. _The earth_ — matter. _The Spirit of God moved_ — motion. All four, in two verses, and the Hebrew carries no sequence: **bara** — He created. One verb. One act. The text does not say "first this, then that," because there is no "then" yet to say it in.

Augustine saw the time-clause eighteen centuries ago: the world was not made _in_ time but _with_ time — "the world was made, not in time, but simultaneously with time." City of God XI.6, c. 426 Time is part of the grant, not the stage the grant happened on. This is the sheet's inheritance from Sheet 000: time is not a fourth thing creation has, but a thing the Trinity does — authored, possessed whole and at once, entered at will. Boethius, Consolation V.6

Part Two — The Paradox, Written Out

## Try to Order Them. You Can't.

Here is the test that makes this sheet more than poetry. Take the four and try to put any one of them first. Every ordering dies — not by decree, but by the meaning of the words themselves.

### Matter First Dies

Matter must be _somewhere_. Content with no place is not waiting for space to arrive — "waiting" and "arriving" already presuppose the rest of the package. Matter first is matter nowhere, which is not matter.

### Space First Dies

Space is the relation-field — the order of situations among things. Relation with no relata is a word, not a place. Leibniz pressed this against Newton's absolute container-space Leibniz–Clarke Correspondence, 1715–16 — and two centuries later general relativity sided with Leibniz: geometry and content co-define. "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve." John Wheeler Neither is prior. They are two grips on one handle.

### Motion First Dies

Motion is change in relation. Nothing moving nowhere along no parameter is not a primitive state of affairs — it is three absences wearing a verb.

### Time First Dies

Time with no change is unmeasurable and undetectable — no clock, no before, no after. Aristotle defined time as "the number of motion with respect to before and after" Physics IV.11, c. 350 BC — the parameter needs the change as surely as the change needs the parameter.

Every ordering is a contradiction, because they are not four things standing in line. They are one thing expressed four ways. Finitude does not enter in stages. It enters all at once.

Part Three — The Signature

## A Trace of the Three in the Fabric of the Four

Stand back from the diagram and notice what kind of structure this is: a set whose members are each constituted through the others, where no member is prior, and where the whole is one act. That is the perichoretic shape — the very structure Sheet 000 drew inside the Godhead, now found stamped into the created fundamentals themselves. Augustine spent half a book hunting for exactly these: _vestigia Trinitatis_ , traces of the Trinity left in what the Trinity made. De Trinitate VI.10; XI, c. 417 He looked in minds and in loves. This one sits deeper — in the grammar of existence itself.

A note on the count: the four debts named in Sheet 000 are in God — internal to how the Fullness accounts for itself. These four faces are in creation — external, the shape stamped into what was made. Two adjacent fours; two different addresses. Do not let them collapse into one.

Held Honestly

### A Vestige, Not a Proof

Augustine's own caution stands: a trace is not a demonstration. The claim of this sheet is structural correspondence — the created four bear the relational signature of the uncreated Three — not that physics derives the Trinity. Sheet 000's floor holds: the ground is not proven from below. What the correspondence does earn is this: the framework expected creation would carry its Author's fingerprint, and the fingerprint is where the expectation said to look — in the parts that cannot be ordered.

The Grammar Note

### Why the Equation Says Ω and Not "Spacetime"

Spacetime is what the four faces look like from inside physics — the L1 instantiation, a manifold with fields on it, still sliced sequentially in the math. Ω, the possibility-space of the grammar, is the four held as one structure — the L2 name for the single expression. The math slices what cannot be sliced, because math is ours and the act is His. The slicing is honest as long as it remembers it is slicing.
