# The Moral-First Chain

Every time we put morals in the middle of the chain, the chain jammed. So we turned the building around. The M-series postulate stack — a working orientation for the whole framework, expanding and contracting as we see. Ratified as direction, July 28, 2026. Not claimed as result.

For a year the framework ran bottom-up: physics on the ground floor, coherence above it, then a bridge into morals, then a bridge into God. Every floor connected by scaffolding, and every paper spent half its mass defending the scaffolding. This sheet records the inversion: **the Good goes to the top, postulated and unashamed, and everything else is derived downward.** Not because it is easier to believe — because it is easier to _prove things_ this way. The same theorems, facing the other direction, carry stronger labels and fewer bridges.

The Old Chain — and Why It Always Jammed

Bottom-up order: **physics → coherence → _bridge_ → morals → _bridge_ → God.** Hume stands at the exact middle floor — you cannot derive _ought_ from _is_ — so the center of the building was a permanent construction site. The is-ought gap, the χ-direction bridge, the measurement-measures-what question, the "188 axioms" confusion: every one of them was an artifact of facing the wrong way.

The gap was never in the world. It was in the direction of the derivation.

What It Costs — The Two Honest Prices, Stated in Advance

**Cost one — Euthyphro comes home** Is the Good good because the source wills it, or does the source will it because it is good? Top-down does not dodge this; it must answer at the primitive level. The framework's answer, already in the corpus: **the Good is the source's nature — identity, not priority.** Stated here as a postulate about M0, so it is never discovered later as an embarrassment.

Audit note — the dissolution's two soft spots (kept visible, per doctrine)

The temporal dissolution carries two load-bearing premises a hostile reader will press, and the paper itself names the test for one of them. **(1) The universal-cost premise:** every moral misalignment carries structural cost, even when undetected — the Gyges-ring case (the fraud that is never found). The paper's answer: the violation exists before, during, and after discovery; deployment only reveals it. Defensible, but it is a premise about _universal_ cost — mark it as such. **(2) Deliberative vs. moral ought:** prospective choice between two futures, neither yet misaligned ("ought I marry?"), may not reduce to misalignment-recognition. The framework's line: deliberative ought is not moral ought. Both premises are stated in PMM §9 as falsification criteria — the paper invites the attack, which is the correct posture.

**Cost two — the problem of evil sharpens before it eases** If the moral is the substrate and physics is its projection, why is the projection so noisy? The answer lives in the Fall/Fracture layer: the noise is introduced, not native.
