# The Plus Sign

Exactly three, or nothing is ever real.

One plus one equals two. But _what does the plus sign do?_ That question has been open in physics since 1932. This paper closes its structural core — and prints the frontier it didn't close.

David Lowe · FP-000 · POF 2828 · August 2026

Part I — Start Here

## One Plus One

Write down: 1 + 1 = 2

Three things are on the page. The **1** on the left. The **1** on the right. And the + in the middle.

The two 1s are objects. They sit there. They do nothing. The + is different. It _acts._ It takes two separate things and produces a third thing — the 2 — that did not exist before the operation ran.

Now ask: **what kind of thing is the plus sign?**

It is not an object like the 1s. It does not sit on either side. It sits _between_ them, and it does something neither of them can do alone. Without the plus sign, you have two separate digits on a page. With it, you have a result.

The plus sign is the simplest possible example of an operation that connects two things and produces something real from their connection.

Hold that thought. Now look at physics.

Part II — The Problem

## Two Theories That Face _Opposite Directions_

Physics has two great theories. Both work. Neither can absorb the other. They have resisted unification for ninety years.

Read structurally, not historically: general relativity faces the future and quantum mechanics faces the past, in the sense of what each equation's variables reference — not in the sense that one theory was discovered before the other or is somehow "older" in kind.

**General relativity faces the future.** Given what exists now, it tells you what will happen. It evolves the state of the universe forward, deterministically, from initial conditions. It is the theory of possibility — the space of what could come next.

**Quantum mechanics faces the past.** After something happens, it tells you what did happen. The measurement record, the Born rule, the projection postulate — all of these update the description of reality based on outcomes that have _already become actual_. It is the theory of actuality — the record of what did occur.

One faces forward. One faces backward. And for ninety-three years, every attempt to unify them has tried to **reduce** one to the other. Make gravity quantum. Make quantum mechanics geometric. Force one direction to swallow the other.

None have worked.

You cannot unify two things that face opposite directions
by forcing one to face the other's way.

You need a third thing. You need a +

Part III — The Plus Sign

## What the Third Thing _Does_

If general relativity generates possibility (facing forward) and quantum measurement records actuality (facing backward), then they meet somewhere. That somewhere is **the present**.

But the present has a strange property: it has no duration. Any interval, no matter how small, has a future side and a past side. The boundary between them has zero width. The present is not a moment you can stand in. It is the _limit_ between two directions.

Something operates at that boundary. Something takes the forward-facing possibility and the backward-facing record and connects them. Something makes the transition from "could happen" to "did happen." That something is the plus sign of physics.

Call it what it does: **actualization.** The operation that makes one possibility into one actuality, irreversibly, at a cost.

The cost is real. Landauer's principle, confirmed experimentally, says that erasing information — collapsing many possibilities into one outcome — requires a minimum energy of kT ln 2 per bit. That energy dissipates as heat. It cannot be recovered. The outcome is locked in. The arrow of time is born.

Time has a direction because actuality has a cost, and costs are irreversible.

So the structure of reality has three parts:

G — Generator (forward-facing, possibility) Physically: unitary evolution, the Schrödinger equation It answers: what could happen? L — Logos / Selector (boundary, structure) Physically: basis selection, the choice of observable It answers: what question is being asked? A — Actualizer (backward-facing, record) Physically: thermodynamic irreversibility, Landauer erasure It answers: what did happen? T = A ∘ L ∘ G One operation. Three functions. Composition, not a chain.

**L is the plus sign.** It sits between the forward-facing and backward-facing theories, and it does something neither can do: it connects them. Without it, you have two separate theories on a page. With it, you have a result.

Part IV — The Elimination

## Why It Has to Be _Exactly Three_

Maybe the structure could be simpler. Maybe it could be more complex. Try every option. Watch each one die.

Contradiction

One function

A single operation both generates and collapses. Generation is reversible. Collapse is irreversible. One operator cannot be both. This is not a limitation of current physics. It is a logical impossibility.

Regress

Two functions

One generates, one collapses. The collapser is itself a physical system. It enters superposition with what it measures. It needs its own collapser. The chain never closes. Two is just a shorter chain.

Regress

Three separate functions

Three independent agents handle generation, structure, and collapse. They need a coordinator. The coordinator is a fourth element. The problem restarts. **A three-link chain is still a chain.** It still points beyond itself. The number alone is not the cure.

Abandons the question

No collapse at all

Every possibility occurs. Nothing is ever selected. The question was: why does one outcome become actual? This answer says: none do. That is not a solution. It is a refusal to solve. Every observer still experiences exactly one result.

Survives

Three functions, one operation

Composition, not observation. Internal unity, not external chain. The third does not _watch_ the second. It _applies_ the second to the first. No element stands outside. No element requires an external verifier. The composition is self-contained. This is the only option that terminates the regress without self-defeating.

The decisive distinction is not between two and three as numbers. It is between **external sequence** and **internal completion.** A chain does not close by becoming longer. It closes by becoming **one.**

That structure has a name. It has had a name for two thousand years.

Christian theology describes a God who is three persons in one being. Not three gods. Not one person wearing three masks. Not three independent agents requiring a coordinator. **Three distinct functions. One indivisible act.** The Father generates. The Son structures. The Spirit actualizes. Not by observing each other — by composing into a single operation.

We did not start from the Trinity and force physics into it. We started from the measurement problem — the deepest structural question in quantum foundations — and the only mechanism that terminates the regress is a triadic unity. The structure that fell out was Trinitarian before we named it.

Part V — Boundaries

## What This _Proves_ and What _Kills_ It

An argument that hides its attackers is not an argument. Here is what this paper claims, what it does not claim, and exactly what would destroy it.

What it proves

The measurement regress requires internally unified triadic closure.

One operator contradicts. Two operators regress. Three separate operators regress. Only a triadic composition — three functions in one operation — closes. This is a structural result. It does not depend on any particular interpretation of quantum mechanics.

What it does not prove

It does not prove that the Trinity is true by physics alone.

The derivation shows that reality requires a triadic self-contained actualization structure. The identification of that structure with the Christian Trinity is a metaphysical bridge: that what physics requires and what theology names are the same thing. That bridge may be correct. It should be stated as the decisive move, not assumed as though the formalism has already completed it.
