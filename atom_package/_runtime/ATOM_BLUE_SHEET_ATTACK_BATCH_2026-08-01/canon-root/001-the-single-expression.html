Part One — The Fullness Speaks

Out of simplicity, everything

Sheet 000 ended with the Fullness — the plērōma, Paul's own word for everything God is, complete, nothing missing. "For it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell." Colossians 1:19; 2:9 Creation begins when the Fullness speaks outward. And the direction of that act settles an old question before it is asked: simplicity breeds complexity. Never the reverse. Fractions do not create unity; parts do not sum to a ground. The simple is prior to the composite — always. Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I q.3 a.7

So the first act cannot be an assembly. It must be an expression — one thing said, that unfolds as many. And that is exactly how the first breath of Scripture reads:

In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.Genesis 1:1–2

Read it with the fundamentals in hand. In the beginning — time, granted first, set apart grammatically from the things made: not an object alongside heaven and earth, but the when He gives for them to run in. The heaven — space. The earth — matter. The Spirit of God moved — motion. All four, in two verses, and the Hebrew carries no sequence: bara — He created. One verb. One act. The text does not say "first this, then that," because there is no "then" yet to say it in.

Augustine saw the time-clause eighteen centuries ago: the world was not made in time but with time — "the world was made, not in time, but simultaneously with time." City of God XI.6, c. 426 Time is part of the grant, not the stage the grant happened on. This is the sheet's inheritance from Sheet 000: time is not a fourth thing creation has, but a thing the Trinity does — authored, possessed whole and at once, entered at will. Boethius, Consolation V.6
Part Two — The Paradox, Written Out

Try to order them. You can't.

Here is the test that makes this sheet more than poetry. On the relational reading, take the four and try to put any one of them first. Every ordering fails — not by decree, but because each term calls for the others.

Split 001.1 — Relation first, but not as settled physics. This sheet treats relation as philosophically primitive and reads space, matter, motion, and time as modes of relation under finitude. That is a strong structural bridge. It coheres with Leibniz, general relativity, and information-theoretic instincts, but physics by itself does not force the whole ontology. Physics may model spacetime as a manifold with fields on it; this sheet asks what that model means in the deeper grammar.

Matter first DIES

Matter must be physically instantiated, located, and dynamically constrained. In the Master Equation grammar, K names meaningful content; in the physics register, matter-energy requires the Standard Model and the relevant field equations. So "matter first" is not killed by a slogan. It fails inside this relational grammar because content with no domain, no relation, and no ordering has not yet become physical matter.

Space first DIES

Space can be read as the relation-field — the order of situations among things. Relation with no relata is a word, not a place. Leibniz pressed this against Newton's absolute container-space Leibniz–Clarke Correspondence, 1715–16; general relativity gives the relational side real force by making geometry and content dynamically coupled. "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve." John Wheeler That does not settle every philosophy-of-physics debate, but it makes the bridge legitimate: space is not merely an empty box in this reading.

Motion first DIES

Motion is change in relation. In the grammar, G·K names ordering acting on content. In physics, motion still requires equations of motion — Euler-Lagrange equations, geodesic equations, or the relevant dynamical law. So "motion first" fails as a primitive here because change already presupposes content, relation, and a parameter of succession.

Time first DIES

Time with no change is unmeasurable and undetectable — no clock, no before, no after. Aristotle defined time as "the number of motion with respect to before and after" Physics IV.11, c. 350 BC — the parameter needs the change as surely as the change needs the parameter.

On the relational ontology, every attempted first-member ordering fails. They are not four things standing in line. They are one created structure expressed four ways. Finitude does not enter in stages. It enters as an integrated grant.

The same figure as Sheet 000's circle, one level down. There: one being, three persons. Here: one act, four faces. The lines are mutual — no arrowheads, because nothing here comes first.

Part Three — The Signature

A trace of the Three in the fabric of the four

Stand back from the diagram and notice what kind of structure this is: a set whose members are each constituted through the others, where no member is prior, and where the whole is one act. That is the perichoretic shape — the very structure Sheet 000 drew inside the Godhead, now found stamped into the created fundamentals themselves. Augustine spent half a book hunting for exactly these: vestigia Trinitatis, traces of the Trinity left in what the Trinity made. De Trinitate VI.10; XI, c. 417 He looked in minds and in loves. This one sits deeper — in the grammar of existence itself.

Held honestly

A vestige, not a proof

Augustine's own caution stands: a trace is not a demonstration. The claim of this sheet is structural correspondence — the created four bear the relational signature of the uncreated Three — not that physics derives the Trinity. Sheet 000's floor holds: the ground is not proven from below. What the correspondence does earn is this: the framework predicted creation would carry its Author's fingerprint, and the fingerprint is where the prediction said to look — in the parts that cannot be ordered.

The grammar note

Why the equation says Ω and not "spacetime"

Spacetime is what the four faces look like from inside physics — the L1 instantiation, a manifold with fields on it, still sliced sequentially in the math. Ω, the possibility-space of the grammar, is the four held as one structure — the L2 name for the single expression. The math slices what cannot be sliced, because math is ours and the act is His. The slicing is honest as long as it remembers it is slicing.

Forces boundary

Ordered relation is grammar; the forces are physics

The four forces are not derived on this page. General relativity, electromagnetism, the strong interaction, and the weak interaction live in the physics register with their own equations and symmetry structures. The Theophysics bridge claim is more modest and more useful: the forces can be read as physical projections of ordered relation. That is structural correspondence, not proof that physics reduces to relation.
