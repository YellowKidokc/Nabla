# Ten Laws Bridge Matrix

Status: First corrective pass  
Created: 2026-07-17  
Purpose: Apply the Bridge Protocol to the Ten Laws so the framework stops relying on ungraded leaps from physics to theology.

---

## What This Fixes

This matrix fixes a framework-level ambiguity:

> The Ten Laws often move from physical behavior to theological meaning faster than the burden of proof allows.

The correction is not to weaken the claims. The correction is to label what kind of claim each sentence is allowed to be.

From this point forward, the Ten Laws should not be treated as one undifferentiated class of claim. Each law has:

- a physics claim
- a structural pattern
- a theological mapping
- a stress point
- a permitted canonical sentence
- an overclaim that should be avoided

---

## Claim Status Key

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strong Bridge | The mapping has clear preserved structure and can carry major framework weight. |
| Promising Bridge | The mapping is useful but needs tighter constraints or source support. |
| Poetic / Homiletic | Useful for teaching or devotion, but should not carry proof-weight. |
| Needs Formalization | The law may be strong, but the exact preserved relation must be defined before using "isomorphic." |
| Overclaim Risk | Current language may imply more than the bridge can support. |

## Immediate Corrections

These corrections should be applied across the canon.

### Replace "Proves" With "Maps" Unless the Claim Is Actually Proven

Avoid:

> Physics proves grace.

Use:

> Physics supplies a measured structure that maps to grace.

### Retire Unguarded "Not Metaphor"

Avoid:

> This is not metaphor.

Use:

> This is more than casual metaphor where the mapping preserves defined structural relations.

### Reserve "Isomorphism" For Formal Mappings

Avoid:

> Gravity is isomorphic to grace.

Use:

> Gravity is a strong structural bridge for grace as persistent drawing.

Only use "isomorphic" after source, target, and preserved relations are explicitly defined.

### Add Stress Points To Every Law

Every law needs a section titled:

```text
Stress Properties
```

That section should name what does not fit, what could be misread, and what the mapping is not allowed to claim.

## What Is Now Fixed

The framework now has a first-pass correction against its main failure mode:

> beautiful mappings becoming overclaims.

The Ten Laws are no longer treated as equally proven, equally formal, or equally safe.

The strongest bridges are:

1. Gravity / Grace
2. Thermodynamics / External Rescue
3. Relativity / Invariant Truth Across Frames
4. Electromagnetism / Truth as Disclosure

The most dangerous bridges are:

1. Quantum / Faith
2. Strong Force / Love
3. Weak Force / Mercy

That distinction matters. It tells the framework where to stand boldly and where to speak carefully.
