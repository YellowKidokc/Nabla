## D.2 The Tolman Problem

The Tolman surface brightness test is one of the most precise observational constraints in cosmology. In an expanding universe, the surface brightness of a distant object should dim as (1+z)^-4, decomposed as:

- Energy redshift: (1+z)^-1
- Time dilation (photon arrival rate): (1+z)^-1
- Bandwidth compression: (1+z)^-1
- Distance duality (angular diameter vs luminosity distance): (1+z)^-1
- **Total: (1+z)^-4**

Observations confirm this scaling to high precision.

The emergent distance framework (main paper) reinterprets expansion as ruler evolution. But a naive application of ruler evolution produces the wrong Tolman scaling. If spatial units and temporal units both evolve as a(t) = 1/(1+z), the emergent framework predicts:

- Energy (spatial unit scaling): (1+z)^-1
- Photon arrival rate (temporal unit scaling): (1+z)^-1
- Angular area (rulers grow, angles shrink): (1+z)^+2
- **Total: (1+z)^0** — flat. No dimming.

This is wrong by four powers of (1+z). The framework cannot survive if it cannot reproduce Tolman.

Where:

- V_horizon = comoving horizon volume

### The Coupling

Time dilation between epochs includes an information-density term:


The exponent 1/3 arises from dimensional analysis: information scales with area (holographic principle: I ~ R^2), volume scales as R^3, so information density scales as R^-1 ~ a^-1, and the cube root maps this to the temporal dimension.

### Revised Tolman Calculation

With information-density time coupling:

- Energy: (1+z)^-1
- Geometric time dilation: (1+z)^-1
- Information time dilation: (rho_info_obs / rho_info_emit)^(-1/3)

- Area: absorbed into distance duality

For the information term to contribute the missing (1+z)^-1 factor, we need:

```
(rho_info_obs / rho_info_emit)^(1/3) ~ (1+z)^-1
```

Which requires:


**Total: (1+z)^-4. Tolman recovered.**
