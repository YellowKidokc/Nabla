## Clean Descent Text

God creates.

Creation stands.

What stands is distinguishable.

What is distinguishable is intelligible.

What is intelligible carries information.

Information permits relation: one thing can stand with, toward, against, or apart from another.

Relation introduces order: relations can be fitting or unfitting, coherent or incoherent, preserved or broken.

Order reveals value: what can be preserved or violated is not neutral.

Value introduces normativity: a relation can be regarded rightly or wrongly.

Normativity becomes moral valence when an agent can knowingly honor or violate the good.

Moral valence requires agency.

Agency requires choice.

Choice requires a world with rules, limits, time, and consequence.

A world with consequence permits vulnerability, loss, decay, and damage.

Damage is not illusion. Disorder is real.

Where order can be refused, adversarial agency becomes possible.

The Enemy is the personal form of refusal: agency turned against coherence, truth, and good.

Adversarial action creates debt: real disorder that cannot be erased by pretending it did not occur.

Perfect Justice requires truth: damage must be answered.

Perfect Mercy wills restoration: the damaged order is not abandoned.

Justice without mercy answers damage without restoration.

Mercy without justice restores by denying truth.

Therefore perfect restoration requires a cost-bearing source that preserves truth while healing damage.

The damaged order cannot be its own sufficient source of restoration.

That external, truth-preserving, restoration-bearing source is Grace.

Grace restores creation toward God without denying the reality of damage.

Christ is the convergence point where justice, mercy, truth, cost, and restoration meet.

The Cross is not an added premise.

It is the place where the descent reaches completion.
