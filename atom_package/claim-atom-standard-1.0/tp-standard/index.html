The Claim-Atom Standard

The unit of scientific record here is the claim, not the paper. Every claim is a permanent, addressable object carrying two author-written layers (plain and technical), a typed classification, a dependency chain, a verification status, and a standing falsification port — the stated condition under which it dies, and the address where challenges register. Papers are narrative tours over claims. Falsify a claim and everything downstream inherits the flag.

The pieces

/vocab/context.jsonld

The vocabulary. JSON-LD context extending schema.org's Claim type with dependency, falsification, verification, and the four edge relations. Includes edge rules and propagation semantics.

/resolver/map.json

The resolver. Logical IDs (tp:A042/L9/C3) map to addresses through this one file — relocate hosting by editing one line, every citation ever minted keeps resolving.

Moral Conservation via Time-Translation Symmetry — three seed claims, one machine-verified in Lean 4, dependency-chained, kill conditions stated.

Honesty rules

Verification badges distinguish kernel-checked proofs from informal derivation — and this site's own claims wear Informal where that is the truth. Unchallenged is displayed as its own status: an untested claim is not a strong claim, and the graph says so. Both layers of every claim are author-written; if they disagree, that is a reportable defect.

This page, the records, and the vocabulary are self-hosted, dependency-free static files. The standard is the JSON-LD; every rendered page is a disposable view. Machine readers: fetch any claim address with .jsonld appended for the raw record.
