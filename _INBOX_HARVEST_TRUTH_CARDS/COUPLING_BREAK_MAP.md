## Short Answer

Coupling breaks when a system has parts, motion, labels, or surface similarity
but lacks the load-bearing relation that makes the parts mutually implicating.

In the current Lean record, the break points are not vague. They are named:

1. **No coupling invariant**: the pieces do not preserve the relation that binds them.
2. **No distinctness**: the pieces collapse into one role.
3. **No dynamics**: the system is static or flattened.
4. **Wrong role profile**: three parts exist, but not the right three functions.
5. **Relabeling without source/profile preservation**: names are swapped while structure changes.
6. **Coupling without entanglement**: contact/activation exists, but not the required binding structure.
7. **Closed-system self-source**: the system tries to power or ground itself from inside itself.

That is the page-two/page-three move: not "three things good." The claim is
"three irreducible, correctly profiled, mutually coupled roles pass; malformed
three-part systems fail."
